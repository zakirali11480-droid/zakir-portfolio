My Leadership Style

Transformational Leadership

Burns (1978); Bass (1985)

My primary leadership identity is rooted in Transformational Leadership theory, first conceptualised by Burns (1978) and later expanded by Bass (1985). This approach emphasises inspiring and motivating teams through a compelling shared vision, intellectual stimulation, and genuine personal connection — qualities that are especially vital within the creative and people-centred world of cultural events.

As a transformational leader, I strive to elevate those around me by fostering creativity, building trust, and cultivating a shared sense of purpose. In an events context, this manifests in collaborative programming, empowering team members to take ownership of their roles, and maintaining a guest-centred vision at every stage of an event's lifecycle.

01

Visionary

Setting ambitious, culturally meaningful goals that inspire teams and stakeholders alike.

02

Inspirational

Motivating through authentic storytelling and a clear sense of shared mission.

03

Intellectually Stimulating

Encouraging creative problem-solving and innovation in event design and delivery.

04

Individually Considerate

Recognising the unique strengths of each team member and nurturing their growth.

Servant Leadership

Greenleaf (1977)

I am also shaped by Servant Leadership principles (Greenleaf, 1977), which place the needs of the team and the community at the heart of leadership practice. In the context of cultural events — which fundamentally exist to serve public audiences — this philosophy is deeply aligned with my professional values.

Cultural Intelligence & Hofstede's Framework

Hofstede (1980); Northouse (2021)

Working across global cultural contexts demands a sophisticated awareness of cultural difference. Hofstede's (1980) dimensions of national culture — including power distance, individualism, and uncertainty avoidance — inform my approach to cross-cultural team management and international audience engagement.
